Endless Metamorphosis

His woodcut Metamorphosis II (1939–40) is a strip about four metres long in which one tessellation turns into the next. Cut in wood, it has to end. Here every edge is a curve shared by the two tiles that meet on it, so the curves can drift with position and time and the strip never opens a gap. It runs forever.

    Filled handles sit on the tile's own three edges; hollow ones are the same edges arriving from the neighbours. Drag either. The neighbours move with it because they read the very same curve, so no drag can open a gap.

    How it holds

    Tile (i,j) owns three edges. Each edge is a spline through 3 control points (t along the chord, y across it), computed once from the morph value at its midpoint. The opposite three edges are the neighbours' copies, read in reverse. Interpolating the control points along x changes the creature without ever changing the topology.
