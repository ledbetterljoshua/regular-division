The Einstein

Escher spent decades on shapes that fill the plane, and every one of them repeated. In 2023 David Smith, a retired print technician from Yorkshire, found a single tile that covers the plane only without ever repeating; weeks later he and his collaborators found the spectre, which needs no mirror images. Ein Stein: one stone. Bend its edge and every stone in the plate changes at once.

The one edge

Drag the two solid knots. Their hollow twins follow, turned half a circle about the centre, so the edge reads the same from either side.

Why it never repeats

Every tiling by this shape is forced to gather into clusters, which gather into larger clusters of the same nine kinds, without end. A repeating pattern is one block of fixed size, copied; here every block sits inside a bigger one, so no block can be the one that repeats. And because each edge carries the same S, a flipped copy cannot meet its neighbours: every stone here is the same stone, only turned.
